# Private channels are a third kind of channel, decided by the same access check

Until now a team channel was open to the whole team, and only a direct message (ADR 0015) could refuse a team member. The team needs channels that keep to some people or keep an agent out. Issue #64 chose private channels on 2026-11-03, and left per-channel permissions (who may post or start a call) until a real need comes up.

A private channel is a channel with `kind: "private"`, a name, and a member list in `channelMembers`, the table direct messages already use. Its creator (`createdBy`) is its first member. Any member can add teammates or agents, any member can leave, and the creator and admins who are members can remove members. Only its members, people or agents, can see it, find it in search or lists, read it, post, react, pin, get alerts from it, start or join its calls, open its shared browsers, or see its attachments, link previews and pins. Everyone else gets `not_found`, team admins included: the room never shows anyone a conversation they aren't in.

The access check stays in one place, `convex/lib/channels.ts`. A channel is a team channel, a direct message or a private channel, and the last two are open only to the members in `channelMembers` (`hasMemberList`). Every path #63 made aware of direct messages follows from that: messages, replies, search (filtered after the search index, as for direct messages), unread and mention counts, `messages.addressed`, pins, attachments, link previews, calls, shared browsers, saved logins, and runs and tasks started there. Where a private channel behaves differently from a direct message, it behaves like a team channel: it has a name, notifies on mentions by default, and sends an A2A agent a run only when the message mentions it.

We chose a third kind over a flag on team channels, so the code that treats a direct message's fixed members specially (opening one again by its members, every member alerted, every A2A agent in it sent each message) can't apply to a private channel by mistake, and so `kind` alone says who may see a channel.

## Consequences

- A channel's kind never changes. No function makes a private channel public, so its history stays private; none makes a team channel private, so a channel's audience never shrinks from under the people in it. Project channels are always team channels.
- `@everyone` in a private channel reaches only its members: the fan-out reads `channelMembers` instead of the team.
- Removing someone from a private channel, or their leaving it, takes them out of it at once: they get `not_found` from then on; every join they had in its calls goes to LiveKit to be disconnected and refused (the same revocation and retry as `calls.removeFromCalls`, page by page, so none is skipped, and never a join they make after being added back); their connections to its shared browsers are cut and their unused browser tickets refused at admission; their views of Spaces on its runs close at once; the wheel of its browsers, and of terminals and Spaces on its runs, goes to someone still in the channel, and a browser nobody left could drive stops. The browser's own channel decides that, so the same happens when an admin outside the channel removes its driver from the team. An agent taken out gets nothing more from runs started there: each is detached, and the A2A worker checks the agent is still in the channel before every step and again right before every send, a plain send after a refused stream included. Removing someone from the team removes them from every private channel, as from every direct message. A private channel nobody is left in is archived.
- Someone added to a private channel sees all of it, its history included, and starts with it read up to the moment they were added. Someone added back starts the same way: nothing from their time away is unread, and their notification level stays. Nothing addressed to them reached them while they were out. Someone who joins the team later isn't in it until a member adds them.
- An agent is a member like a person: an agent key with `room.read` reads no private channel its agent isn't in. Creating a private channel and adding members need the `work.manage` scope; leaving needs `messages.post`. The person accountable for an agent can hold its keys, so adding an agent also lets that person read what the agent reads.
- Runs and tasks from a private channel get the treatment ADR 0015 gave runs from a direct message, and tasks from a direct message's call get it too, as does a task a private run is filed under (`runs.file` never mixes private and other work). A run sent from a message there is titled "Message from Ana in a private channel"; a task accepted from a proposal in its call keeps its channel in `tasks.directChannelId`; a run started on that task inherits it. Everyone else sees them without their texts (`private: true`, a title that names no channel and never one a bridge reported, no approved action), and only the channel's members can act on them. A task's owner, and an A2A agent sent its work, must be members. The field keeps the name `directChannelId`, which dates from ADR 0015, because renaming it would remove a field from the schema.
- The activity log leaves out entries about a direct message or private channel the viewer isn't in, an agent's own calls there included, so the log doesn't give away what happens in one or that it exists. Each entry keeps the channel it is about from when it was written (`audit.channelId`), so deleting the message doesn't make its entries public; an entry about a terminal, a Space, or something in one, follows its run to the channel.
- Terminals and Spaces on a run from a private channel are that channel's too: listing them, reading their output and input, a Space action's result, and every control need membership.
- Attachments in a direct message or private channel have no lasting link, which changes what ADR 0015 said for direct messages. `messages.attachmentLink` gives a member a link for ten minutes (`attachmentGrants`), and `/attachments/grant/<token>` serves it only while that member can still see the channel, so removal ends every link they hold. The time is checked in the HTTP action, not the query, because Convex caches query results. Team channels keep their links.
